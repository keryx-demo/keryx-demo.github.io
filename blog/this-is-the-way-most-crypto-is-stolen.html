This is the way most crypto is stolen

security · 2026-03-25T14:00:00Z · Trezor Team

While we were in the middle of filming our latest video, we got word that scammers were at it again — impersonating us in a massive blast to Trezor users. The trick here is all about "manufactured urgency." You get an email that looks official and insists you act right now. The scammer is betting that the fear of losing your money will make you panic and follow their instructions without stopping to think, eventually handing over total control of your wallet.

The solution is actually pretty simple: never, under any circumstances, share your wallet backup with anyone. If you can stick to that one rule, you'll probably never lose your funds to a scam. Red flags to watch for: fake update links, fake devices on random sites, fake websites and search results, targeted phishing and spoofing, and threats of "blocking" or "deactivating" — a hardware wallet cannot be blocked or turned off remotely.
